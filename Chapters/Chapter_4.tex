\chapter{Acquired Skills During Industrial Training}
\label{chapter-4}

\section{Introduction}
\par The industrial training at Active Green Energy Ltd. provided hands-on experience in business analysis, requirements engineering, UI/UX design, and AI-assisted frontend prototyping within the Digital Solutions department, over a period of approximately 130 hours from 23 July to 23 September 2026. This chapter summarizes the technical and soft skills developed during the training and reflects on how this experience bridged academic learning with practical industry application.

\section{Learning New Technologies and Applying Them to Solve Engineering Problems}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Jira:} Learned to use Jira for day-to-day task tracking and coordination across the two assigned projects, progressing from basic ticket updates to actively organizing my own workload against project deadlines.
    \item \textbf{Figma (Prototyping):} Developed intermediate proficiency in Figma for translating documented requirements into user flows, wireframes, and interface designs, used as the design reference for both key projects.
    \item \textbf{Claude Code and MCP-based AI Development Tools:} Learned to use Claude Code (with the Model Context Protocol) and Antigravity IDE as AI-assisted development tools, converting approved Figma designs into interactive frontend prototypes, while always reviewing and validating AI-generated output against the original requirements rather than accepting it directly.
\end{itemize}

\section{Theoretical Knowledge Gained}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Requirements Engineering:} Applied classroom knowledge of functional and non-functional requirements analysis to real client requirements, learning to distinguish clearly stated needs from implicit assumptions.
    \item \textbf{System Diagramming:} Used Data Flow Diagrams (DFD), Use Case Diagrams, and Entity-Relationship (ER) Diagrams -- concepts covered in coursework -- to formally document the two industrial monitoring systems worked on during training.
    \item \textbf{SDLC and Agile/Scrum:} Applied academic understanding of the Software Development Life Cycle and Agile/Scrum methodologies to recommend suitable development approaches for each project based on its requirements and constraints.
\end{itemize}

\section{Practical Knowledge Acquired}

\par Beyond the core business analysis and design work, this internship gave me a practical understanding of what it takes to run a small, technology-enabled solutions team -- knowledge that would be directly transferable to starting a similar venture of my own.

\par If I were to start a startup similar to Active Green Energy Ltd.'s Digital Solutions division, it would likely take the form of a small digital-solutions consultancy focused on helping industrial and SME clients digitize a single operational pain point at a time -- for example, a lightweight energy or production-monitoring dashboard -- rather than attempting a large, all-in-one platform from day one. In terms of resources, the minimum viable team would need one technical co-founder or contracted developer to complement a business-analysis/design-focused founder, along with access to standard hardware (laptops and cloud hosting) rather than heavy capital equipment. From a budgeting perspective, the biggest lesson from this internship is that AI-assisted prototyping tools such as Claude Code can meaningfully reduce the time and cost of producing a working, demonstrable prototype before committing to full development, which would allow a very small team to validate a project with a client before investing in a larger build. A realistic first project type would be a fixed-scope pilot -- for instance, a single-dashboard monitoring tool for one client process -- used to build a track record before pursuing larger, multi-module industrial systems.

\section{Communication Skills Developed}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Client Communication:} Participated in client meetings to gather and validate requirements, progressing from an observational role to more direct involvement in explaining proposed solutions.
    \item \textbf{Technical Reporting:} Prepared and presented SRS/PRD documentation and system diagrams to my supervisor and, at times, to clients, learning to communicate technical concepts clearly to both technical and non-technical audiences.
    \item \textbf{Design Presentation and Feedback:} Presented UI/UX designs and prototypes to stakeholders, collected feedback, and iterated designs accordingly -- a cycle that improved my ability to explain and defend design decisions.
    \item \textbf{Project Communication:} Used Jira and Slack for ongoing task coordination, status reporting, and feedback communication with the wider team.
\end{itemize}

\section{Collaboration Skills Enhanced}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{With Designers and Developers:} Worked closely with the UI/UX designer and software developers to accurately describe requirements, track progress against deadlines, and ensure all agreed scope items were addressed.
    \item \textbf{With Testers:} Collaborated with the software tester to help confirm that test cases aligned with the documented requirements before sign-off.
    \item \textbf{Pull Request Review Sessions:} Attended pull request review sessions with the development team to help verify that no required features were missing and that documented constraints were fulfilled.
\end{itemize}

\section{Ethical Responsibilities and Professional Conduct}
\par Professional conduct was particularly important when communicating unfavorable news to clients. During the IIoT-Based Production Monitoring System project, a scheduled factory visit by the IoT engineering team was delayed, which in turn delayed device setup and affected the development timeline. Following my supervisor's guidance, I communicated the situation to the client clearly and professionally, explaining the reason for the delay without placing blame on any individual or team, and discussed the resulting impact on the timeline. Maintaining transparent, non-defensive communication in that situation reinforced the importance of professional integrity, even when the news itself was not positive. \section{Conclusion}
\par This training developed both technical and interpersonal skills central to business analysis and digital solution design -- from requirements documentation and system diagramming to AI-assisted prototyping, client communication, and cross-functional collaboration. It also reinforced the professional and ethical responsibilities that come with representing a technical team to a client. Together, these experiences have prepared me for future roles in business analysis and digital product development.
