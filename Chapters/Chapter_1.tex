\chapter{Introduction}

\par \textbf{Industrial Training} serves as an essential link between academic knowledge and professional work experience. It provides students with the opportunity to apply theoretical concepts learned in the classroom to practical, real-world situations within an organizational setting. This hands-on exposure helps students develop not only technical skills but also important soft skills such as communication, teamwork, and time management, which are vital for success in the professional environment.

\par The primary purpose of industrial training is to prepare students for the workforce by familiarizing them with workplace culture, industry practices, and operational procedures. It enables students to face real challenges, learn problem-solving techniques, and understand the expectations of a professional setting. By engaging in actual projects, interns can bridge the gap between academic theories and industrial applications.

\section{Problem Domain}

\par This industrial training report documents my experiences and learning outcomes during my internship at \textbf{Active Green Energy Ltd.}, a Dhaka-based technology and industrial energy solutions provider. During this period, I was involved in the \textbf{Digital Solutions department}, working primarily on business analysis, requirements engineering, and UI/UX prototyping for industrial digital products. As a trainee, I encountered challenges such as unfamiliarity with the company's internal workflow and documentation standards, translating incomplete or informally communicated client requirements into structured technical documents, and coordinating between non-technical stakeholders and the development team. Support from my industrial supervisor, Md. Mochaddec Hossain (Business Analyst), and the rest of the Digital Solutions team helped me overcome these challenges and successfully contribute to the assigned projects.

\section{Motivation of Training}

\par The motivation behind pursuing industrial training includes:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Professional Development:} Gaining practical experience and developing relevant skills aligned with the business analysis and digital product development industry.
    \item \textbf{Career Exploration:} Understanding the day-to-day realities of working as a business analyst within a technology-driven organization.
    \item \textbf{Application of Knowledge:} Applying academic theories in requirements engineering, system analysis, and software development life cycle (SDLC) concepts to real client work.
    \item \textbf{Skill Enhancement:} Improving technical, analytical, and interpersonal skills through direct exposure to client meetings, documentation, and prototyping work.
    \item \textbf{Adaptability:} Learning to work effectively within a dynamic and evolving workplace, including hybrid remote/on-site collaboration.
    \item \textbf{Networking:} Building professional relationships with supervisors, developers, designers, and clients, and receiving mentorship from an experienced business analyst.
    \item \textbf{Career Foundation:} Preparing for future employment in business analysis and digital solution design by gaining hands-on experience and industry insights.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope of Training}

\par The scope of this industrial training encompassed activities directly related to the Digital Solutions department, including business analysis, requirements documentation, UI/UX design, and frontend prototyping for two industrial digital product concepts: an Industrial Energy and Machinery Monitoring System, and an IIoT-Based Production Monitoring System. It included hands-on project work, client requirement-gathering meetings, participation in Jira/Slack-based task coordination, and use of industry-standard tools. Areas outside the core project scope, such as backend development, hardware/IoT device implementation, and unrelated departments (e.g., Machinery Solutions, Administration), were not covered. This focus ensured concentrated skill development and meaningful contributions within the assigned domain.

\section{Identifying the Gap Between Academia and Industry}

\par There is often a notable gap between academic learning and industry requirements. Common causes include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Theoretical Knowledge vs Practical Application:} Academic curricula cover requirements analysis and SDLC models in theory, but offer limited exposure to the ambiguity and constant revision typical of real client requirements.
    \item \textbf{Limited Access to Industry Tools:} Students may not have hands-on experience with tools such as Jira, Figma, and AI-assisted development environments that are widely used in the industry.
    \item \textbf{Work Environment Dynamics:} Academic projects usually lack the collaborative, multidisciplinary teamwork -- involving business, design, and engineering stakeholders -- required in professional settings.
    \item \textbf{Standards vs Real-World Constraints:} Industry environments impose deadlines, resource limitations, and changing requirements that are not fully covered in academic programs.
\end{itemize}
